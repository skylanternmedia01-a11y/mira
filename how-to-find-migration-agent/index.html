How to Find a Good Migration Agent in Australia

Finding the right migration agent can feel overwhelming — there are over 5,000 registered agents in Australia, and it is hard to tell who genuinely specialises in your visa type. Here is a practical guide to finding an agent you can trust with one of the most important applications of your life.

Start with MARA Registration

Every legitimate migration agent in Australia must be registered with the Office of the Migration Agents Registration Authority. You can check any agent's registration status on the official MARA register. If someone offers immigration advice and is not registered, they are operating illegally. This is your first and most important filter — never use an unregistered agent, regardless of how confident they seem or how cheap their fees are.

Look for Visa-Specific Experience

Migration law is broad. An agent who primarily handles student visas may not be the best choice for a complex partner visa application. Ask potential agents what percentage of their caseload involves your visa type. An agent who handles your visa category daily will spot issues that a generalist might miss. They will also have a better sense of current processing trends and Department expectations for your specific application type.

Red Flags to Watch For

Be cautious of agents who guarantee visa approval — no agent can guarantee an outcome. Avoid agents who pressure you to sign immediately, who are vague about their fees, who cannot explain their strategy in terms you understand, or who suggest dishonesty in your application. A good agent is transparent about your chances, upfront about costs, and willing to tell you when your case is weak.

Compare Multiple Agents

Never hire the first agent you speak to. Get quotes from at least 2–3 agents who specialise in your visa type. Compare their experience, their fee structure (fixed vs hourly), what is included in their fee, and how they communicate. The cheapest agent is not always the best value — and the most expensive is not always the most competent. What matters is relevant experience and clear communication.
