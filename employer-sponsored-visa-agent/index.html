Employer Sponsorship Visa Pathways

The main employer sponsored pathways are the subclass 482 (Temporary Skill Shortage), the 186 (Employer Nomination Scheme — permanent), and the 494 (Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional — provisional). The typical pathway is 482 first for temporary work rights, then transition to 186 for permanent residency after 2–3 years. Some occupations and employers qualify for direct entry to the 186 without the temporary stage. The new Skills in Demand visa (replacing the 482) introduces salary-based thresholds — your agent needs to be across these recent changes.

Why Both You and Your Employer Need This Right

Employer sponsored visas require your employer to become an approved sponsor, lodge a nomination for your specific role, and meet training benchmarks and salary requirements. Many employers have never sponsored before and need guidance on their obligations — including record-keeping, salary compliance, and notification requirements. An experienced agent manages the employer side while simultaneously preparing your visa application, ensuring both streams align and don't create delays.

Skills Assessment and Labour Market Testing

Depending on the pathway, you may need a skills assessment from the relevant authority and your employer may need to demonstrate labour market testing — evidence that they could not find a suitable Australian worker for the role. The requirements vary by stream, occupation, and international trade obligations. An agent who regularly handles employer sponsorship knows the current exemptions and requirements and can prepare both sides efficiently.
