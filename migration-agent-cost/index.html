How Much Does a Migration Agent Cost in Australia?

Migration agent fees vary significantly depending on the visa type and complexity of your case. Here is a realistic breakdown of what you should expect to pay, what should be included, and how to spot agents who overcharge or underdeliver.

Fees by Visa Type

Student visas (subclass 500) typically cost $800 to $2,500. Partner visas (820/801, 309/100) range from $2,500 to $6,000. Skilled visas (189, 190, 491) cost $2,000 to $5,000. Employer sponsored visas (482, 186) range from $2,500 to $6,000. Parent visas cost $3,000 to $7,000. Business and investment visas (188, 888) cost $5,000 to $15,000 or more. These are agent fees only — government application charges are separate and often substantially higher.

What Should Be Included

A good agent's fee should include an initial eligibility assessment, preparation and review of all application documents, lodgement of the application, handling of requests for additional information, and communication with the Department throughout processing. Ask specifically whether skills assessment assistance, health and character evidence guidance, and post-lodgement support are included or charged separately. Get the fee agreement in writing before you commit.

Fixed Fee vs Hourly Rate

Most migration agents charge a fixed fee for standard visa applications. This is generally better for you because you know the total cost upfront. Some agents charge hourly rates for complex or unusual matters — if so, ask for an estimate of total hours and a cap. Be wary of agents who charge hourly for standard applications — this creates an incentive for inefficiency.

When Cheap Is Expensive

The cheapest agent is not always the best value. An underqualified agent who lodges an incomplete application can cause months of delays, requests for additional information, or even a refusal — costing you far more in lost time and reapplication fees than the difference between a cheap and competent agent. Judge agents on experience and communication, not just price.

Frequently Asked Questions

Some agents are flexible on fees, particularly for straightforward cases or multiple visa applications (e.g., partner visa plus visitor visa for family). It is reasonable to ask, but be cautious of agents who discount heavily — quality work takes time.

Many agents offer payment plans — typically an upfront deposit followed by staged payments. Ask about payment terms before signing. Avoid agents who require full payment before starting any work.

Migration agent fees are generally not tax deductible for individuals. However, employer-paid sponsorship fees may be deductible for the business. Consult a tax professional for your specific situation.

Your fee agreement should outline the scope of work and total cost. If additional work is needed (e.g., a ministerial intervention after refusal), the agent should discuss the additional cost before proceeding. Unexpected charges without prior agreement are a red flag.
